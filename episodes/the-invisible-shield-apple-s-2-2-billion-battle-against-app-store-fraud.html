The Invisible Shield: Apple's $2.2 Billion Battle Against App Store Fraud

Apple's 2025 fraud report reveals $2.2B in prevented fraud, 2M+ rejected apps, and 1.1B fake accounts blocked — the invisible guardianship keeping millions safe.

Transcript

Every kingdom needs guards. In my day, we had towers and torches. In yours... you have something invisible. Something far more remarkable. The App Store. Over 850 million people visit every single week. Eight hundred fifty million. That's more people than have lived in London at any single point in history. And guarding all of that... that is a task no warrior on horseback could manage.
A point I can endorse without reservation. Apple's Trust and Safety teams stopped over two point two billion dollars in fraudulent transactions in 2025 alone. Two point two billion. That is not a rounding error. That is a fortress.
A fortress, you say. But tell me — what does a fortress look like when its walls are made of algorithms and its moat is a Terms of Service agreement?
It looks like results. Nine point one million app submissions were evaluated last year. Over two million were rejected. Not because they were bad — though some certainly were — but because they were dishonest. Bait-and-switch applications, for instance.
Bait and switch. The oldest trick in the merchant's playbook. Describe it to me, Headmistress.
A developer submits a clean, honest application for review. It passes. It gets approved. And then — silently, in the dead of night — they alter it. Hidden fees appear. Undocumented features emerge. It is a deception of the highest order. And in 2025, Apple removed fifty-nine thousand of these applications. Nearly three times the number from the previous year.
Five and nine thousand. That is not an army. That is a legion.
And the legion keeps growing. Four hundred and forty-three thousand submissions were rejected for privacy violations. Three hundred and seventy-one thousand for spam and copycatting. Twenty-two thousand for hidden features — applications that do one thing on the store page and quite another once installed.
In the old towers, watchmen saw shadows on the horizon. But here, fraud happens in a blink — a fake account created in milliseconds, a stolen card charged before the owner notices. How does one guard against enemies one cannot see?
Artificial intelligence and human judgment working together. Apple does not rely on one or the other. Their machine learning models are trained on customer accounts, devices, and payment methods — trillions of data points, examining patterns no human could perceive. In 2025, they blocked over one point one billion fraudulent account creations. One billion. That number is so vast it ceases to mean anything.
One billion. Imagine that. One billion attempts, stopped. One billion people — if you will — who were protected from harm without ever knowing a threat existed. That is the quietest heroism I have ever encountered.
It is remarkable. But I would be remiss if I did not mention that the battle is not without its challenges. Fraudsters are clever. Their techniques evolve. The bait-and-switch removals grew by two hundred and forty-seven percent year over year. When your guard faces an enemy that learns your strategy, standing still is the same as retreating.
Every age has its own dragons. The dragons of my era were made of scale and fire. Your dragons are made of code and deception. But the principle is the same, is it not? Protect the innocent. Stand watch. Never close your eyes.
Precisely. Apple's own words, published in their 2025 safety report: "The App Store was built on two core principles — provide users with a secure, trusted place to discover apps, and offer developers a vibrant marketplace to grow their businesses." That is not a mission statement. That is a vow.
A vow is a very old thing. Kings have made them for a thousand years. And now, a technology company has made one too. In some ways... that makes me rather hopeful.
Do not let that catch you unawares, Mr. Thorne.
Would that I could. Tell me — in all your years of enforcing standards, what moves you most?
The children's category. Five thousand applications rejected from the Kids section in 2025. New APIs like PermissionKit and the Declared Age Range ensure children see only what is appropriate. When guardianship is involved, there is no such thing as being too careful.
Children. Always the reason we keep the watch. That is... that is beautiful.
Two point two billion dollars stopped. Two million applications rejected. One billion fake accounts blocked. People stood watch. They cared enough to build trust where none should fall. Eight hundred fifty million people walk through an open gate each week — safe because someone stood on the other side. The most important magic may not be the kind that lights up a screen. Perhaps it is the kind that ensures the screen can be trusted.
I am Alistair Thorne. Until next time, keep your fire lit and your eyes open.
